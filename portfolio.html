Every wedge's angle is its current value, and its core hue comes from the position's name — cyan at the a's, violet at the z's. Portfolios are separated by the black radial lines, not by colour. The inner disc's area is the second figure: for a position that gained, the name-tinted core is the purchase value and the light green band out to the rim is the gain. A position that lost is drawn the same way, only outwards: its light core runs out to the rim as the current value and the light red band beyond the rim — area-true to the purchase value — is the loss. Wedges worth at least 0.3% of the total are labelled where the column has room; the table below lists every position. Outside all of that, a green band shows realised gains from sales (area-true, same scale); a dark red band shows a realised loss.
Each tile is one position, its area proportional to the position's current value — which portfolio it sits in plays no part in the layout — hover a tile for that. Colour is the unrealised return — green for a gain, red for a loss, grey at zero, saturating at +300% and −100% along a hyperbolic curve — it spends its contrast where the returns actually live, so 10% → 20% shifts the colour as far as 50% → 150% does. The bar across the foot sits flush against the body — no divider — and its height is the result measured against the tile it sits in: gain ÷ current value, so a +100% position fills half the tile, and a −50% position, whose loss equals what is left, fills the whole one. Body and bar share the one ramp: the body takes the total return, the bar the annualised one, so a long-held position with a big total gain but a modest yearly rate shows a bar lighter than its body, and a recent flyer shows a darker one. The annualised figure is a CAGR from the position's first activity date — Parqet exposes no per-trade cash flows, so it approximates IRR and understates positions bought in several tranches. Hover a tile for the exact figures.
