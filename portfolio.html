Every wedge's angle is its current value, and its core hue comes from the position's sector — the same colours the map's sectors use, listed in the legend below. Wedges are ordered by value alone, regardless of which broker holds them — hover a wedge to see that. The inner disc's area is the second figure: for a position that gained, the name-tinted core is the purchase value and the light green band out to the rim is the gain. A position that lost is drawn the same way, only outwards: its light core runs out to the rim as the current value and the light red band beyond the rim — area-true to the purchase value — is the loss. Wedges worth at least 0.3% of the total are labelled where the column has room; the table below lists every position. Outside all of that, a green band shows realised gains from sales (area-true, same scale); a dark red band shows a realised loss.
Each tile is one position; its size is the position's current value, so bigger always means worth more today, regardless of which broker holds it — hover a tile to see that. Colour is the unrealised return — green for a gain, red for a loss, grey at zero — but it stops getting more intense past +300% or −100%, so two big winners (or losers) can look almost the same colour even when one has actually done much better. The area across the foot is a second figure for the same tile: its height is the gain or loss as a share of current value, so a +100% position's area fills half the tile, and a −50% position's — where the loss equals everything that's left — fills the whole tile (losses should logically sit outside the current value box, but that would increase the total portfolio area). The body colours by total return, the foot area by annualised return (XIRR) — the money-weighted rate implied by the position's own trade dates and amounts — so a long-held position with a big total gain but a modest yearly rate shows a lighter foot than body, and a recent fast mover shows the opposite. The rare position with too little trade history for XIRR to solve falls back to a plain CAGR from its first activity date instead. Hover a tile for the exact figures.
Hue top to bottom, saturation left to right. Picking a cell saves it in this browser, so it stays picked here even after a reload — but it won't follow you to another browser or machine, which still see registry/sector_colors.csv's own colours.
